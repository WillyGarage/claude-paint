# Dalí: the proposed box

Source keys are those of `notes/research/dali_materials.md`. Tiers: 1 =
documented in use by analysis, his own words or a witness; 2 = in his
working materials (a paint box or palette he used, repeated purchases);
3 = a one-off purchase nothing contradicts. Only one painting of his (1946)
has a published pigment analysis, so most tier-1 rows rest on his own
words in the 1948 treatise. Those are marked "own words": the treatise is
prescriptive, and the one examined painting contradicts it once (strontium
yellow). No tier-2 or tier-3 evidence was found: no paint box, palette or
purchase record of his turned up.

## New tubes

### aureolin

- **Pigment.** Potassium cobaltinitrite (cobalt yellow) [AP1 p.37].
- **Why new.** Documented in the 1946 painting and the only yellow he
  names for glazing ("spreading aureolin over Naples yellow") [ALE p.66;
  50S]. No catalog tube is a cool, transparent, glazing yellow: Indian
  yellow is transparent but warm and organic; the cadmiums and chromes are
  opaque.
- **Masstone** `#d4a52a`. *Estimate.* A tint of it with titanium white in
  oil is Munsell 3Y 7.2/10, "strong yellow" [AP1 p.38]; the masstone is
  set darker than that tint and a little greener than the catalog's Indian
  yellow.
- **Hiding** 0.2. Direction sourced: "relative transparency in oil
  mediums," "can also serve as a glaze in oil" [AP1 p.38]; "it is
  transparent" [SAL §20]. The number is an estimate, just above Indian
  yellow's 0.15.
- **Stiffness** 0.4. Direction sourced: it needs "6 parts oil to 4 parts
  dry powder" [AP1 p.40], so a soft, oily paint. The number is an
  estimate.
- **Tinting strength** 0.7. *Estimate.* Its refractive index is only
  moderate, 1.72–1.76 [AP1 p.38]; no measured strength was found.
- **Drying** `COBALT_BLUE` 1.4. *Estimate*, following the catalog's rule
  that cobalt pigments are siccative in oil (as for cobalt violet).

### blue black

- **Pigment.** Vine (charcoal) black: carbon from charred vine twigs, with
  no bone phosphate [SAL §290].
- **Why new.** The treatise makes a working distinction between two
  blacks: ivory black for the underpainting and blue black "for the song,"
  the last layers; glazing them the other way round makes grays "dirty and
  pasty" [50S]. The 1946 canvas has a black layer of carbon alone besides
  a gray with phosphate [ALE pp.69, 72]. Bone black (charred bone, warm,
  strong) cannot play the cool role.
- **Masstone** `#1d1f25`. Direction sourced: "cold, with an imperceptible
  blue tinge" [50S]; "a cool neutral tint" [SAL §290]. The value is an
  estimate.
- **Hiding** 0.85. *Estimate*: carbon blacks cover well; set just below
  bone black's 0.9 for its weaker body.
- **Stiffness** 0.6. *Estimate.*
- **Tinting strength** 0.8. Direction sourced: "of weaker body than ivory
  or lamp black" [SAL §290], so below bone black's 1.1. The number is an
  estimate.
- **Drying** `LAMP_BLACK` 0.35. *Estimate*: a pure carbon black, like lamp
  black; no period rate for vine black was found.

## Left out

- **Titanium white (anatase).** Found only in the ground of the 1946
  canvas, mixed with calcium sulfate, calcite and talc, which reads as a
  maker's preparation [ALE pp.68–72]. No paint layer has it and the
  treatise never names it. Grounds here can be laid with lead or zinc
  white.
- **Fillers.** Gypsum/anhydrite, calcite, talc, barium sulfate, potassium
  sulfate and clay minerals [ALE pp.66–69]: extenders, not colors.
- **Massicot (lead monoxide).** One Raman find with lead white in a gray
  [ALE p.68]; probably a drier or a by-product of the lead white.
- **Chromium oxide green, anhydrous.** In the copied list as "celadon
  green" and one other anhydrous chromium oxide [50S]. The 1946 "chromium
  greens" are not said to be anhydrous in the sources read, and his own
  Secret 34 is the hydrated form. Worth adding if the full 1946 paper names
  it.
- **Colors only in the copied list of permanent colors** (the colorman's
  voice, not his): light red, Mars red, Mars brown, brown ochre,
  transparent brown, Cassel earth, real ultramarine and the French
  synthetic ultramarine (both covered by the ultramarine tube), peach
  black. He also turns away
  from Indian red ("your passion will leave you no room to love Indian
  red") [50S]; it is in the box only as a stand-in for Mars violet.
- **Emerald green (copper aceto-arsenite).** On the copied list of
  unsuitable colors with the other copper greens [50S p.141]; his one
  "emerald green" is mapped to viridian (above).
- **Burnt umber.** Banished from the palette "as categorically as bitumen"
  and kept only for the transfer paint of a tracing [50S]; there is no
  transfer step at this easel.
- **Green earth.** Allowed "only rarely" [50S]; no instance found.
- **Banned in the copied list** [50S p.141]: raw umber, bitumen, mummy,
  ivory brown, violet lake, cochineal carmine, carmine lake, roasted
  madder lake, redwood lake, chrome yellow, Indian yellow, zinc yellow, Prussian blue,
  Schweinfurt, Paris and Scheele greens, raw sienna by name (it enters the
  box only as the proxy for Italian earth).
- **Strontium yellow as its own tube.** Lemon chrome, a pale chromate
  yellow, is close enough; the pigment rests on one painting and the
  treatise bans it.
- **Lamp black.** Named only in a grinding note copied into the treatise
  [50S].
