# Miró: the proposed box

Source keys are those of `notes/research/miro_materials.md`, plus [AP3]
below. Tiers: 1 = documented in use by analysis, his own words or a
witness; 2 = in his working materials (studio tubes, repeated mentions
in letters); 3 = a one-off purchase nothing contradicts. A color named
only in a letter that may be an order, not a description of use, is
counted as tier 2 when it recurs and tier 3 when it does not. "Read
second-hand" means the analysis is known only from the pigment table in
[GL23] (Table 1), which summarizes [SAL03] (1935), [OD06] (1938) and
[BURN91] (1949).

## New tube

**titanium white**

- **pigment:** titanium dioxide (anatase or rutile; early artists'
  grades were often composites on barium sulfate [AP3 pp.297–299])
- **masstone:** `#f5f5f2`. Estimate: a neutral white, cooler than the
  catalog's lead white (`#efe9dc`) and a shade above zinc white
  (`#f3f3ef`).
- **hiding:** 0.95. Direction sourced, value an estimate. Hiding power
  rises with refractive index, and titanium dioxide's is far above lead
  and zinc white's [AP3 p.309, fig. 6]; even the first composite had
  "remarkable hiding power" [AP3 p.297]. Set above lead white (0.82)
  and zinc white (0.6).
- **stiffness:** 0.6. Estimate, with a sourced comparison: oil
  absorption of rutile is 15–20 and anatase 20–24 lb per 100 lb of
  pigment, against 13–22 for zinc oxide [AP3 p.315, Table 6], so the
  same body as zinc white (0.6).
- **tinting strength:** 2.0. Direction sourced, value compressed:
  measured tinting strengths are about 1,000–1,900 for rutile and
  1,200–1,300 for anatase, against about 140 for lead white and 170 for
  zinc white [AP3 p.309]. The literal ratio (about 8×) would swamp
  every mixture; the catalog compresses Prussian blue's "very high"
  strength to 3.0 the same way. Estimate.
- **drying:** `ZINC_WHITE` (0.35). Direction sourced: titanium dioxide
  does not react with the oil, which gave "soft films that were slow to
  dry, particularly in oil media"; anatase dries faster than rutile [AP3
  pp.309–310]. The choice of the zinc-white rate is an estimate (both
  are slow, unreactive whites).

Why it is needed: it is found by analysis in two of his oils (1938, 1949)
and is the white of his 1970s vinyl paint, and no catalog white comes
near its hiding and tinting strength.

## Left out

- **Lamp black.** Documented only in the vinyl paint of two 1973 black
  grounds and in an enamel can of 1965 [GL23 pp.22–25]. His one oil black
  was ivory black, including the black oil ground of 1974 [GL23 p.26].
  A lamp-black oil tube dries badly [SAL §294], the opposite of the
  fast vinyl he used it in. Bone black covers his oil blacks.
- **Phthalocyanine ("monastral") green.** One canvas of 1949, known
  only from a table summarizing an unpublished thesis [GL23 Table 1;
  BURN91]. It would need a new tube on single, second-hand evidence; his
  other greens are viridian and "cadmium green," a viridian–cadmium
  yellow mix. Worth adding if the thesis is read.
- **Cobalt yellow (aureolin), zinc yellow.** Same 1949 canvas only
  [GL23 Table 1]; no tubes or mentions.
- **Lithopone.** Same 1949 canvas only [GL23 Table 1]; a weak white
  (tinting strength about 280 [AP3 p.309]) between zinc and titanium
  white, which cover it.
- **Cadmium green** (26 tubes). A mixture of cadmium yellow and viridian
  [GL23 p.16]; mix it from pale cadmium or cadmium yellow and viridian.
- **Strontium yellow, "yellow orange."** Named once, in the 1920 letter
  [GL23 Table 1]; tier 3, no tube, no analysis.
- **Chrome yellow.** He rejected it in 1918 as "dull and lightless"
  [HS23]. A 2025 scan found chromium in some Mallorca yellows but named
  no pigment [MAL25].
- **Green earth** ("terra verte naturale," five tubes in the second
  studio, whose stock is mostly unopened [GL23 pp.15–16, Table 1]). Tier
  2 only, with no mention or analysis.
- **Cassel earth** (one Spanish-brand tube [GL23 Table 1]). No use
  documented, and no close catalog tube.
- **Unknown names.** A green sold under a Venetian painter's name (1918,
  1941–42), a green and a brown named after a Barcelona paint maker
  (1941–42), "bitumen black," "brown black" [GL23 Table 1]: pigments
  unknown.
- **Tar, sand, casein, enamels, vinyl and acrylic paints.** Media and
  matter, not tube colors; tar and sand belong to the 1936 hardboard
  paintings [MAS].
- **Barite, magnesium carbonate, kaolin.** Extenders in his preparation
  and tubes [FJM25; HS23; GL23 p.27], not colors.
- **Yellow ochre.** Not documented.
