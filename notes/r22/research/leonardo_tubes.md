# Leonardo da Vinci: the proposed box

Source keys are those of `notes/research/leonardo_materials.md`; the
sources are repeated at the end. Tiers: 1 = documented in use by
analysis, his own words or a witness; 2 = in his working materials (a
paint box, a palette, repeated purchases); 3 = a one-off purchase that
nothing contradicts. No paint box, palette or run of purchase records
survives for him, so every row below is tier 1. The weakest tier-1 row is
bitumen, which rests on his own words and no analysis.

**Proxies.** *Rose madder* stands for a red lake whose dyestuff the
sources read do not name; the tube's transparency and slow drying fit
the documented glazes. *Bone brown* stands for the translucent brown earth: it is the
catalog's semi-transparent, slow-drying dark brown, which is how that
earth behaves (its medium-rich underpaints caused drying defects [VOR
p.43]). Raw umber, the other candidate, is opaque and a fast drier (2.4),
the opposite. The tube's own pigment, roasted bone, is not documented for
him.

## New tubes

Columns as in `palette.rs`: masstone, hiding, stiffness, tinting
strength, drying.

### azurite

- **Pigment:** basic copper carbonate, the natural mineral, ground.
- **Why new:** documented by cross-section in the sky, the mantle
  underpaint, foliage and draperies of the Virgin of the Rocks [VOR pp.36,
  39, 45–46]. No catalog tube is a copper blue; cobalt blue and smalt are
  different pigments, and cobalt blue is a nineteenth-century one.
- **Masstone `#2a5684`.** Estimate. Direction sourced: azurite "has a
  greenish undertone" where ultramarine is purplish [AP2 p.26]; coarse
  grinding gives the darker blue painters used under ultramarine [AP2
  p.25].
- **Hiding 0.55.** Estimate. Direction sourced: refractive indices
  1.730–1.838 [AP2 p.26], far above linseed oil and ultramarine's 1.50
  [AP2 p.42], so in oil it covers better than the ultramarines (catalog
  0.15–0.3). Coarse particles (largest "often ... in the 20-30 µ range"
  [AP2 p.26]) keep it below the opaque earths.
- **Stiffness 0.45.** Estimate: a coarse, gritty paint.
- **Tinting strength 0.4.** Direction sourced: ultramarine's tinting
  strength is "much better than that of other early blue pigments such as
  azurite or smalt" [AP2 p.44]. Value an estimate, just under smalt (0.45).
- **Drying `COPPER` 1.6.** Direction sourced: copper pigments "exert both a
  siccative and an antioxidant effect on an oil film" [AP2 p.27]. The
  value is the catalog's copper constant; estimate.

### natural ultramarine

- **Pigment:** lazurite extracted from lapis lazuli, a full-strength
  (first-extraction) grade.
- **Why new:** documented in the Virgin of the Rocks, the Madonna of the
  Carnation, the Lady with an Ermine and the Mona Lisa [VOR pp.39, 45–46;
  EARLY p.13; KRK; GON23 pp.77, 82]. The catalog's "ultramarine blue" is
  the synthetic pigment, first made in 1828 [AP2 p.55], so not one he
  could have used; "ultramarine ash" is the pale last extraction [AP2
  p.39], not the deep blue of his mantles.
- **Masstone `#1f276e`.** Estimate. Direction sourced: in oil "it appears
  very dark blue in a thick layer" [AP2 p.44] and has a "slightly
  violet-blue hue" [AP2 p.40]; set a little duller than the synthetic
  tube (`#232a8c`) because the natural pigment carries colourless mineral
  impurities [AP2 p.42] (inference).
- **Hiding 0.25.** Direction sourced: "a translucent glazing color when
  used in oil" [AP2 p.44], and synthetic grains "appear more opaque than
  those of the natural pigment" [AP2 p.42]; so below the synthetic tube's
  0.3. Value an estimate.
- **Stiffness 0.5.** Estimate, as the catalog's ultramarines.
- **Tinting strength 0.8.** Direction sourced: tinting strength "quite
  good" [AP2 p.44], but diluted by "appreciable amounts of crystalline
  mineral impurities, mainly colorless" [AP2 p.42]; so below the synthetic
  tube's 1.1. Value an estimate.
- **Drying `ULTRAMARINE` 0.8.** Direction sourced: ultramarine is among
  the "poor-drying blue pigments" in oil [AP2 p.119]. The catalog's
  ultramarine constant.

### charcoal black

- **Pigment:** charred wood (vegetable carbon).
- **Why new:** the blacks analysed in his paint are charcoal and carbon
  black [VOR pp.43, 45–47]; no bone or ivory black is reported. The
  catalog's only black is bone black, which differs in hue and strength:
  wood charcoal is "of weaker body than ivory or lamp black" and "of a
  cool neutral tint" [SAL §290], while ivory black has "a slight tendency
  to brown in its pale washes" [SAL §293]. His greys (the grey priming,
  the charcoal underpaints of the draperies, the charcoal in his flesh
  shadows [VOR pp.43, 45, 47]) depend on that cool, weak black.
- **Masstone `#26282b`.** Estimate; direction from "cool neutral tint"
  [SAL §290], against bone black's warm `#1e1b19`.
- **Hiding 0.8.** Estimate (a little under bone black's 0.9; no source).
- **Stiffness 0.55.** Estimate.
- **Tinting strength 0.6.** Direction sourced: "of weaker body than
  ivory or lamp black" [SAL §290]; below bone black's 1.1. Value an
  estimate.
- **Drying `BONE_BLACK` 0.4.** Direction sourced: blacks "by themselves
  never dry," in a French account of 1660 quoted at [AP2 p.136]. The
  catalog's bone-black constant; estimate.

**Fallbacks** if a new tube is refused: charcoal black → bone black (the
weakest of the three cases); natural ultramarine → ultramarine blue (an
anachronism the materials note would have to flag); azurite has no fair
proxy in the catalog.

## Left out

- **Synthetic ultramarine ("ultramarine blue"), cobalt blue, cerulean,
  Prussian and Antwerp blue, cadmiums, chromes, Mars colours, zinc white,
  viridian, emerald green, cobalt violet, magenta, permanent alizarin,
  Indian yellow, transparent oxide yellow, Naples yellow (lead
  antimonate):** made after his time or not
  documented for him. The 1802 translation's "Naples yellow" is its word
  for giallolino, lead-tin yellow [GON23 pp.84–85; TREAT ch. CCXXX].
- **Ultramarine ash:** the pale last extraction [AP2 p.39]; no evidence he
  used it apart from full-strength ultramarine.
- **Bone black:** not reported in his paintings; his blacks are charcoal
  and carbon black (above).
- **Smalt, pale smalt:** one note on grinding smalt [NB §728]; not found
  by analysis in the panels covered by the sources read.
- **Green earth:** his note "The shadow will be burnt terra-verte" [NB
  §623] is a roasted green earth, not the catalog's raw one; an EDX
  reading of rock paint "might suggest a green earth-type composition"
  [VOR p.55 n.23]. Too weak for a tube.
- **Orpiment, realgar:** a yellow recipe [NB §621], and his doubt that
  greens made with burnt orpiment last [NB §626]; no analysis found them;
  no catalog tube.
- **Saffron, turmeric, aloe (organic yellow glazes):** his own recipes
  [NB §§618, 626; TREAT ch. CCXXIX]; fugitive, not found by analysis, no
  catalog tube.
- **Indigo:** found under ultramarine in a panel by his teacher, not in
  his own [EARLY p.8].
- **Copper resinate (transparent copper green):** copper in the shadow
  glazes of some faces [VIG] was read only through search excerpts and
  not identified as a compound; copper green covers verdigris glazes.
- **Lead oxide (litharge, massicot):** a drier in his oil (found in his
  Milan wall painting, inferred in the Mona Lisa's ground) [GON23
  pp.82–86], not a colour; no tube.
- **Raw and burnt sienna:** no evidence; his "burnt ochre" is mapped to
  red earth.
- **Malachite:** no evidence found.
- **Chalk, starch, gold and tin leaf:** an extender, a lake extender and
  gilding [VOR pp.46–47]; not paint tubes.
